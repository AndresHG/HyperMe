# Phase 0 speed test, 25 November 2026: summary

The gate did **not** pass. The best setup reached a median of 1110 ms and a 90th percentile of
1290 ms from key release to cleaned text, against 800 ms and 1200 ms. Language was right on
every sample in every run, and no sample failed.

This is a first result, not the full gate run: 24 Spanish and 14 English samples instead of
25 and 25 (`es-01` and `en-15` to `en-25` were not recorded).

## What the numbers say

- **Cleanup was the first bottleneck.** With OpenAI it took about 0.8 s to its first token
  and missed the 1500 ms deadline on 17 of 76 runs. Groq cut that to about 0.3 s after the
  transcript, with no fallbacks.
- **Speech is now the bottleneck.** The final transcript arrives 0.7 to 0.8 s after release,
  which is the whole 800 ms budget on its own. `--stt-delay minimal` saved about 65 ms at
  the median.
- **The network to OpenAI looked slow.** The warm round trip to `api.openai.com` was 733 ms
  in run 1, against about 200 ms to `api.groq.com` in runs 2 and 3. The two were not
  measured side by side, so a rerun on another
  network is needed before drawing a conclusion.
- **Cleanup quality.** The owner has not judged it yet. A read of the texts by Claude, which are not in the repository, found
  Groq `gpt-oss-20b` at effort low lighter than `gpt-6-luna`: it left 20 of 76 texts
  unchanged (against 6 of 59 for `gpt-6-luna`), often kept fillers, and once kept a
  mistranscribed word that `gpt-6-luna` fixed from context. No translations and no added
  commentary.

## Earlier attempts the same day, not counted

- The first key stored in the keychain was truncated to 128 characters by the interactive

  stores the key from the clipboard.
- The first full run with a working key was stopped after 30 of 76 runs: the new OpenAI
  account allowed 2 requests a minute on `gpt-live-transcribe` and 7 runs failed with
  `rate_limit_exceeded`. After the account moved to the Build tier (2,000 requests a minute)
  the run was repeated as run 1 above.

## Next

Ways to close the gap, cheapest first. None is decided.

1. Repeat run 3 on another network or wired, to settle the network question.
2. A faster speech provider for streaming: the research shortlist in
   `docs/research/2026-11-24/01-dictation.md` (Soniox, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs, Speechmatics).
3. A larger fast cleanup model (Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b`) or a tighter prompt, to recover
   quality without losing the time.
4. Clean the text in pieces while the user is still speaking, so little is left after
   release.
5. Record the missing 12 samples and time Wispr Flow on the same Mac for a direct comparison.
